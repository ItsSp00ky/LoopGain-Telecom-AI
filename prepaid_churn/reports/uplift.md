# T17 uplift experiment

Generated by `uv run --script experiments/uplift.py` (ticket T17).

T11 gives a retention bonus to the customers this module ranks riskiest, and assumes a share of them are saved by it.
This asks the question that assumption depends on: on data where a treatment was actually randomised, does ranking by risk find the customers a treatment changes?

## The two datasets

| Dataset | Rows | Features | Treated | Response rate | Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Orange Belgium | 11,896 | 178 | 76% | 96.57% | a retention call, measured as customers kept |
| Criteo 10% | 1,397,959 | 12 | 85% | 4.69% | an advertisement, measured as site visits |

Orange's response is retention, so 96.57% means 3.43% churn; Criteo's is a site visit.
Both are real randomised trials, which is the only kind of data an uplift number can come from.
Neither is committed: they are other people's data under non-commercial licences, fetched into `data/external/` on demand.

## Ranking by uplift against ranking by risk

Measured on a held-out 30% of each dataset, with both rankings fitted on the same training rows.
Qini is normalised by the perfect ranking; uplift at 30% is the realised difference in response rates inside the top of each ranking.

| Dataset | Ranking | Qini | Uplift at top 30% |
|---|---|---|---|
| Orange Belgium | by uplift (two-model) | -0.0081 | -0.41% |
| Orange Belgium | by risk, as T11 does | 0.0078 | +2.15% |
| Orange Belgium | random | 0.0014 | +0.86% |
| Criteo 10% | by uplift (two-model) | 0.0698 | +2.89% |
| Criteo 10% | by risk, as T11 does | -0.1138 | +0.02% |
| Criteo 10% | random | -0.0011 | +1.12% |

The random row is the mean of twenty uninformative rankings, and their spread is the band a result has to clear: Orange Belgium +0.0014 plus or minus 0.0105 (two standard deviations, over 3,569 held-out rows with 120 non-responders); Criteo 10% -0.0011 plus or minus 0.0110 (two standard deviations, over 419,388 held-out rows with 399,625 non-responders).

Agreement with `sklift.metrics.qini_auc_score` on the same rows: Orange Belgium 1.46e-03, Criteo 10% 8.14e-06.
The Qini here is written out rather than imported, because the control arm has to be rescaled to the treated arm's size at every depth and that term is invisible inside a library call; the cross-check is what keeps it honest.

## What this means for T11

On Orange Belgium, ranking by uplift reaches a Qini of -0.0081 against 0.0078 for ranking by risk, and the realised uplift in the top 30% is -0.41% against +2.15%. Both sit inside the noise band of plus or minus 0.0105, so this dataset answers nothing: 3,569 held-out rows holding 120 non-responders cannot separate one ranking from another, and picking a winner from these two numbers would be reading noise.

On Criteo 10%, ranking by uplift reaches a Qini of 0.0698 against -0.1138 for ranking by risk, and the realised uplift in the top 30% is +2.89% against +0.02%. The uplift ranking clears the noise band of plus or minus 0.0110 and the risk ranking does not; they are not the same ranking, and here the risk ranking is worse than random.

The practical reading for T11: the customers most likely to leave are not the same customers an offer keeps, so a 'share saved' applied to the riskiest decile is an assumption about persuadability that the risk model does not measure. T11 already holds out a random group of proposed customers, and that holdout is the only thing in this module that can turn the assumption into a measurement. Until it has run against a real campaign, every LYD figure downstream of it stays a scenario, which is how the retention report already presents it.

## Limits

- Neither dataset is this module's population. Orange is postpaid, its features are anonymised principal components, and nothing in it can be joined to ours. Criteo is advertising, not telecom.
- So the transferable finding is about the method and the ranking, never a number to paste into T11's policy.
- The upGrad data this module trains on has no treatment arm at all, so no uplift model can be fitted on it. Measuring this properly needs the operator to run a campaign with a real control group, which is what T11's holdout is for.
- The two-model difference is the simplest uplift learner and the least efficient: both models spend their capacity on the response, and the uplift is what survives in the difference. A direct uplift learner would be the next thing to try, and it is only worth trying once there is a real campaign to fit it on.
