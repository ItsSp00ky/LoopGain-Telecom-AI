## Guardrails and no-offer outcomes

Already-silent, unscored, low-risk and held-out customers get no proposal.
The cap is per customer in this campaign, not a cumulative annual retention budget.
Current bundles are T18's behavioural mapping, not observed Almadar subscriptions.
A customer mapped to a monthly bundle above the configured base cannot receive a cheaper unlimited product, regardless of churn risk.
5G and shared-family products are excluded because device, coverage and membership eligibility are absent; catalogue presence alone does not establish eligibility.
A bonus must include at least one service the customer uses; data-only offers require data use, and voice-only offers require voice use.
The morning product's 06:00-11:00 restriction is stated in both reasons; the export cannot establish time-of-day preference or incremental usage.
Each customer first gets the highest-net-value feasible candidate; ties prefer the configured bonus then sorted offer ID.
Only positive candidates are considered; greedy allocation sorts net value descending and subscriber ID on ties, skipping candidates the remaining budget cannot fund.
This is a greedy allocation, not a proof of the optimal knapsack solution.

The untargeted baseline gives every feasible active, scored, non-holdout customer the same inclusion probability: targeted spend divided by their total offer cost.
It includes low-risk and nonpositive-value candidates to represent treating everyone.

## Review and holdout

A seeded SHA-256 lottery assigns approximately the configured holdout fraction independently of input order and batch size; those customers never get an offer.
Keep the campaign snapshot and holdout assignment for a future measured campaign.
No response outcomes or treatment effects are generated here.
`churn decide` writes proposals and an empty released campaign.
`churn approve --proposals <proposals.json> --reviewer <name>` resolves all pending proposals; repeat `--subscriber-id` to select individual pending proposals.
Use `--reject` to reject and `--note` for a review note.
Use `--refresh` with a reviewer name to rebuild derived files after an interruption without deciding any remaining proposals.
Only approved rows enter `released.csv`; it omits internal risk and value fields.
Reviews record subscriber, reviewer, UTC time, decision, note and campaign fingerprint.
Files provide local integrity and review bookkeeping, not authenticated identity; T15 must add access control before a shared service can approve or expose offers.
No offers are sent or provisioned by these commands.
